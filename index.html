Dead Balls Deserve a Second Bounce.

Keep your old tennis balls out of the landfill and give them a new life right here in Bloomington.

Find a Drop-Off Near You

How It Works

1. Collect

Save your flat, dead, or worn-out tennis balls.

2. Drop Off

Bring them to a local public park or partner bin.

3. Recycle

We process them into new materials, avoiding local landfills.

Our Mission

We ensure that every collected tennis ball finds a meaningful second life. Here is where they go:

1. Recycled Materials

We ship them to specialized facilities that grind them down to create new materials like tennis court surfaces and turf.

2. Animal Shelters

We donate playable balls to local animal shelters so rescue dogs have plenty of toys to play with while waiting for their forever homes.

3. Nursing Homes

We provide them to local nursing homes where they are placed on the bottom of walkers to help seniors move smoothly and safely.

Why It Matters

300M

tennis balls are discarded globally each year.

That's a mountain of non-biodegradable rubber sitting in our landfills for over 400 years. Our local courts bring us together; let's make sure the way we treat the game protects our home, too.

About the Founder

I’m a junior at Bloomington High School South, and I’ve been playing tennis for nearly my whole life. After countless practices and matches, I couldn't ignore how many dead tennis balls were just ending up in the trash. That’s why I started Second Bounce back in the summer of 2025. What started as a personal project is growing into a community effort. My goal is simple: make recycling our tennis balls as easy and common as stepping onto the court.

— Will

Second Bounce

A community initiative founded at Bloomington High School South dedicated to keeping tennis balls out of our local Bloomington landfills.
